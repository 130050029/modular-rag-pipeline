The Great Barrier Reef is the world's largest coral reef system, stretching over two thousand kilometers off the coast of Queensland, Australia. It is composed of thousands of individual reefs and hundreds of islands, and it supports an extraordinary diversity of marine life including fish, mollusks, sharks, rays, and sea turtles that migrate through the region every year. Rising ocean temperatures have caused repeated coral bleaching events over the past three decades, threatening the long-term survival of the reef ecosystem and the coastal communities that depend on tourism and fishing revenue generated by the reef each year. Scientists continue to monitor water temperature and coral health closely across hundreds of monitoring sites distributed along the length of the reef system.
