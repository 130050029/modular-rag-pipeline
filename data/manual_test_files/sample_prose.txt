The Amazon rainforest spans nine countries in South America and covers over five million square kilometers. It is home to an estimated ten percent of all known species on Earth, including thousands of tree species, mammals, birds, and insects found nowhere else. The forest plays a critical role in regulating the global climate by absorbing large amounts of carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Deforestation driven by agriculture, logging, and mining has reduced the forest's size significantly over the past fifty years, raising concerns among scientists about long-term ecological stability and the loss of biodiversity.
